GFTV HelloEvents

Privacy

Last updated 27 November 2026

GFTV HelloEvents is run by Global Furry Television (GFTV). This page says what it keeps about you, who sees it, where it goes, and how to delete it. It covers the website at events.globalfurry.tv and the Android app, which shows the same website.

The short version

  • No ads, and nothing about you is sold or used for advertising.
  • You can look at events without an account. To RSVP you need one: a username, a display name, an email address (to sign in, and for sign-in codes only if you turn them on) and a password.
  • Organisers see what their event asks you for (such as a phone number or a registration form), and never your email address.
  • ID checks are only for events that ask for one. HelloID, GFTV's own, deletes your ID photos as soon as it decides, and keeps only what stops one person verifying two accounts.
  • Delete my account on your account page removes your account and everything tied to it.
  • The home page, event pages and the "not found" page use Google Analytics to count visits. Pages where you sign in or type details don't.

1. Looking at events

You can browse and open events without an account. The home page, event pages and the "not found" page load Google Analytics, which counts visits (which pages are opened, roughly where from, and on what kind of device and browser) and uses cookies to tell visits apart. Your browser's settings or an ad blocker can stop it, and the site works the same without it.

2. Your account

There are two kinds of account. GFTV staff sign in with their gftv.asia account, which is run by GFTV separately. Anyone else signs up here, and we keep:

  • Your username, display name and email address. The email address is used to sign in, and nobody else sees it. We email you only if you confirm your address on your account page (section 8), and then only sign-in and password reset codes and a note when your password is reset. Never news or adverts.
  • Your password, only in a scrambled form (a bcrypt hash), which can't be turned back into the password.
  • Your country of origin, which cross-border events need.
  • Your legal name, date of birth and an emergency contact, only if you add them, for events whose registration form asks. The legal name, date of birth and country lock once saved, so nobody swaps who they are between events; a GFTV admin can correct them after checking who you are.
  • Your notification choices, and the Telegram sticker packs you add, if you edit events.

Usernames and display names that look like GFTV's team (staff, admin and so on) are refused, to stop anyone pretending to be us.

3. RSVPs, tickets and registration

When you RSVP, we keep what the event asks for and what you choose:

  • whether you come to the whole event, just for a meal or just to lepak, and an RSVP ID for that event;
  • if the event asks: your contact details (such as a phone number, Telegram or Discord username), whether you are a fursuiter, whether you would like to volunteer, and its registration form's answers, which can include a photo (such as a con badge photo);
  • for a paid ticket: the tier, the amount, how you paid, a payment reference if you give one, and the screenshot of your payment.

Payment itself happens outside this site (by the organisers' link or QR code, or on the day). We never see your card or bank details. Photos and payment screenshots are kept private, and deleted when they are replaced, when your RSVP goes, or with the event or your account.

4. What organisers see

An event's organisers (GFTV staff, and anyone an admin makes an editor of that event) see its attendee list: your display name, username, RSVP ID and what you come for, and only what that event asks you for (contact details, form answers and photos, payment, country for a cross-border event, your account details if its form asks for them, and whether you passed its ID check). They never see your email address or password.

GFTV's admins, and the organisers' own Telegram chats if they set them up, get a message about each new attendee and each payment: your name, username, RSVP ID, what you come for, your ticket and payment, and your contact details. Never your email address or your form answers. These messages are kept in those Telegram chats.

5. ID checks

Some events ask people to verify their ID before they RSVP. Most don't. You verify once, on your account page, and it counts for every event that accepts the way you used. Passing sets your legal name and date of birth on your account, which then stay locked. One document, or for HelloID one face, can verify one account only.

HelloID

HelloID is GFTV's own check, done on GFTV's server; nothing is sent to an outside service. You take a photo of your ID (and its back, for a card), then a short video check of your face, turning your head while the screen flashes colours. The server checks that your face is live and matches the ID, reads a passport's machine-readable zone (the lines of <<<) for the name and date of birth, and looks at your device and connection: your browser and system, your camera's name, your time zone, your screen size, and the country your connection comes from.

  • If every check is clear, it is approved at once and the photos are deleted straight away.
  • If not, the photos are kept, privately, until a GFTV admin looks at them and decides, and then deleted. You are told the decision, and the reason if it is declined.
  • After an approved check, GFTV keeps only: a face template (a list of numbers made from your face, not a picture, stored encrypted), a code made from your document's country and number (not the number itself), a code made from your device, the name and date of birth it was for, and when it was decided. They are only used to stop the same person verifying another account, and are deleted with your account.
  • Checks that are declined or never finished are deleted after 30 days.

Didit and Veriff

Some events also accept Didit or Veriff, outside identity services. If you choose one, you go to their page, and they handle your ID photos and face check under their own privacy policies (Didit, Veriff). We tell them only a code for your account. From them we keep whether you passed, the name and date of birth on the document, and a code made from the document's country and number (not the number itself), so the same document can't verify another account.

6. Check-in and entry codes

When you are going to an event, its page shows your check-in QR code. Organisers scan it, or type your RSVP ID or username, at the door, and we keep when you were checked in, by whom, and how. If the event checks ID at the door, we also keep that an organiser checked it, and when. With self check-in, you scan a QR code the organisers show, which checks you in from your own phone.

So check-in works without a connection, the organisers' check-in page keeps the attendee list on their device until a day after the event: names, usernames, RSVP IDs, payment status and, if the event checks ID, legal names and dates of birth. They can clear it sooner.

Some organisers give their own entry QR codes, such as cinema tickets. The one for you is shown on the event page, to you only.

7. Notifications and Telegram

  • Browser notifications, if you turn them on: your browser gives us an address to send them to, kept until you turn them off. Notifications go through your browser maker's push service (such as Google's, Mozilla's or Apple's).
  • Telegram, if you link it: we keep your Telegram chat ID and username, to send you sign-in codes and, if you ask, event notices. Telegram handles the messages under its own privacy policy.

We only send messages about events you are going to (changes, reminders and the like), new events if you ask for them, and what your account needs. Nothing else.

8. Sign-in, security and misuse

  • Signing in sets one cookie, which keeps you signed in for 7 days. We store only a scrambled form of it.
  • Two-step sign-in, if you set it up: an authenticator app's secret (encrypted), passkeys (their public keys only), and backup codes (scrambled).
  • Email codes, if you confirm your address: which address you confirmed and when. Each code and link is kept only in a scrambled form and expires after 10 minutes. A sign-in link also sets a cookie for 30 minutes, so it works only in the browser that asked for it.
  • Cloudflare Turnstile checks that a person, not a bot, is signing in, signing up or resetting a password.
  • Misuse limits and the audit log: to slow down attacks and keep a record of important actions (such as sign-ins and changes to events), we keep a scrambled form of your IP address with them. Rate limit counts are deleted after a day, and audit records after a year.

9. What stays on your device

Your browser keeps your theme and language, and, for events you are going to, your pass (your check-in QR code and any entry code) so it opens with no connection. The site's offline copy of its pages is kept by your browser too. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes all of it.

10. The Android app

The Android app opens this website, so everything on this page applies to it in the same way. It asks for the camera only when you use HelloID or scan QR codes at check-in, and for notifications only if you turn them on. It doesn't use your location, contacts, microphone or advertising ID.

12. Keeping and deleting your data

Your account and what's tied to it are kept until you delete them. To delete your account: open your account, the Profile tab, then Delete my account. You'll be asked for your password. Then your account, RSVPs, check-ins, photos, payment screenshots, ID check records (including HelloID's face template), two-step sign-in, Telegram link and notification addresses are all deleted straight away. This can't be undone.

Staff accounts belong to gftv.asia; ask GFTV to remove one. Can't sign in? Email us from the address on your account, and we'll delete it for you.

Taking back an RSVP deletes it, with its photos and payment screenshot. An organiser removing you from their event does the same. Audit records stay for up to a year with your name taken out.

13. Your choices and rights

You can browse without an account, and leave out the details an event doesn't ask for. You can change your display name, emergency contact and notifications on your account page, unlink Telegram, and turn notifications off.

Depending on where you live (for example under Singapore's PDPA, or the UK or EU GDPR), you may have the right to see the data we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, take a copy, or object to how it's used. Email us to ask, and we'll answer within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

14. Keeping it safe

Everything between your device and the site is sent over HTTPS. Passwords, backup codes and sign-in cookies are stored only in scrambled forms, authenticator secrets and face templates encrypted, and photos, screenshots and entry codes privately: only the people this page names can open them. The database's keys stay on the server. No service is perfectly secure, but if something goes wrong that affects your data, we'll tell you.

15. Children

GFTV HelloEvents isn't made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly keep their data. If you think a child under 13 has made an account, email us and we'll delete it.

16. Changes to this page

If what GFTV HelloEvents does with your data changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top says when.
